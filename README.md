# I combined 103 public indicators across Toronto’s 140 historical neighbourhoods. Here are some of the patterns that emerged

This project combines 103 public indicators across Toronto’s 140 historical neighbourhoods to explore how the city’s demographic, socioeconomic, health, housing, transportation, infrastructure, public-safety, and civic characteristics relate to one another. After harmonizing dozens of datasets into a common neighbourhood geography, I used correlation analysis, hierarchical clustering, dimensionality reduction, regression, and K-means clustering to identify broader patterns in the data and groups of neighbourhoods with similar profiles.

The results highlight interconnected patterns involving factors such as income, immigration, density, health, voter turnout, housing, and public services, while also showing how dramatically neighbourhoods can differ across multiple dimensions at once. The analysis is exploratory rather than causal: its goal is to reveal structure in a complex collection of public data, visualize Toronto’s neighbourhood diversity, and provide a transparent technical framework for investigating the patterns further.

Full technical writeup is available here: https://epheva.github.io/toronto-neighbourhood-correlations/
